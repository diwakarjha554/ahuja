# Buyer's judgement: four brand directions for Ahuja PA World

Judged as two buyers at once: a school trust secretary who has bought PA for 20 years (conservative, spec and GST and warranty first, wants to phone a human before releasing a sanctioned budget), and a 28 year old event rental operator in Noida (mobile and WhatsApp first, price and loudness per rupee, buys line array and trolley speakers in sets). The test each time: in the first five seconds, and before trusting the site with INR 60,000, does it read as a real, genuine Ahuja dealer who will not misquote a spec.

Grounding done: all four direction documents read in full; AUDIENCE.md read in full; the old logo viewed (the manufacturer's cobalt block "AHUJA" with a red "PRO", plus the red sound wave favicon, which all four correctly refuse to reuse); and five product shots viewed (DPA-570M matte black amp with silver knobs and a red LED segment display, on monstera leaves; ALX-11000 dark charcoal line array with a prominent cobalt Ahuja badge and blue rigging, on marble; BTA-880 black trolley with two grey wireless mics, on wood; AM-30SS white and petrol blue megaphone, on wood by a window; CSD-6303T white ceiling speaker with silver mesh, on wood). Hardware palette confirmed: matte black, silver and grey grilles, small emissive red LEDs, a cobalt blue Ahuja badge, and several white units. Backgrounds are cluttered and every direction correctly mandates re-masking to clean white.

## Honesty check on the contrast numbers

I re-ran ten pairs across the four decks with the WCAG 2.1 relative luminance formula. Every one matched the document's stated ratio to two decimals:

- Institutional: accent fill #D97706 on #FFFFFF = 3.19 (claimed 3.19, and honestly labelled fill only, never text); text primary #16202E on #F7F8FA = 15.44 (claimed 15.44).
- Stage: amber link #F5A524 on #14171C = 8.80 (claimed 8.80); text muted #828B99 on #1E232B = 4.59 (claimed 4.59); sidebar active #F5A524 on #11151B = 8.97 (claimed 8.97).
- Heritage: brand link #9E2B28 on #FFFFFF = 7.42 (claimed 7.42); text muted #6C6456 on #F5F0E6 = 5.14 (claimed 5.14).
- Technical: accent #B45309 on #FFFFFF = 5.02 (claimed 5.02); brand #1456B8 on #FFFFFF = 6.88 (claimed 6.88); text muted #616876 on #ECEEF1 = 4.82 (claimed 4.82).

No direction inflated its numbers. The only honest marginals are Stage's text muted at 4.59 and Technical's text muted on sunken at 4.82, both of which still pass 4.5.

## 1. Specified by institutions  (navy and graphite, marigold signal, light storefront, dual mode admin)

- audienceFit 8. Excellent for the high value, repeat, spec-driven core: schools, offices, banks and hospitals, integrators writing a BOQ, and government procurement, which together are five of the nine personas and most of the rupees. The 100 V tap tables, wattage matcher, dense spec tables and GST clarity answer exactly what AUDIENCE.md asks for. Light storefront matches the bright marketplace that price aware Indian buyers expect. The honest weakness, which the deck admits, is that navy and graphite lean cold for worship committees, shopkeepers and individual buyers who respond to warmth; the single marigold signal and warm photography only partly offset this.
- differentiation 6. Navy plus one warm accent plus spec tables is a competent but well worn institutional B2B look; it will not be mistaken for the pirated Botble store, but it also will not feel unmistakably ownable. The rising level meter mark is a good, honest device.
- accessibility 9. Thorough and honest. Marigold is documented as fill only because it fails text contrast on white at 3.19, which is the correct call rather than a hidden flaw. Full light storefront plus light and dark admin tables, focus ring specified at 2px plus 2px offset.
- buildability 7. The most token surface to maintain: a light storefront set plus a dual mode admin set, three families (Archivo, Inter, JetBrains Mono) plus Noto Sans Devanagari. The deck itself flags drift risk across the token sets.
- notAiLooking 8. Concrete voice rules with real model numbers and phone numbers; permitted flat line coverage and 100 V wiring diagrams are a genuinely useful, human touch. Slightly generic as a system, but never AI slop.
- adminFit 9. The strongest admin story: an explicit operator dark mode for long back office sessions, a specified 256px sidebar with marigold active bar, and 36px dense data grid row actions that stay off the storefront.
- Total 47.

## 2. Sound you can feel  (dark charcoal storefront, amber accent, light and dark admin)

- audienceFit 6. The confident dark stage flatters the aspirational gear (line array, trolley speakers) and will appeal to the Noida event operator, but it fights the rest of the audience. Price aware, mobile first Indian buyers are trained by Amazon, Flipkart, IndiaMART and the old store to read white as the safe, real shop; a dark store risks a first impression of unfamiliar or flashy rather than genuine for the conservative school secretary and the "original hai kya" individual. The deck honestly names this. The central production tension is roughly fifty white background catalogue photos sitting in light wells on charcoal, which looks like a patchwork of white rectangles unless the well is policed perfectly.
- differentiation 8. Distinctive and self assured; clearly nobody's pirated CMS and clearly its own thing.
- accessibility 8. Honest and mostly strong, but dark body risks amber-on-dark fatigue if discipline slips, the muted pair is a marginal 4.59, and the storefront contrast block cites a #C0392B danger value that is not in the storefront table.
- buildability 6. The heaviest build: three token sets (dark storefront, light admin, dark admin), three families plus Noto, and the light well consistency must be enforced by a single component or the grid degrades.
- notAiLooking 7. Copy rules are concrete and good, but dark hero plus amber leans toward a trendy tech startup register that is a half step toward the generic. The single 3px ember rule under an h2 is tasteful and is not an eyebrow (it sits below, not above, the heading).
- adminFit 7. Light default admin plus dark option is good, but the amber-on-light admin introduces separate ink tokens (#8A5212, #9A560F) to keep straight.
- Total 42.

## 3. Heritage, sold properly  (cream paper, Fraunces serif, brick red, brass trim)

- audienceFit 5. The weakest fit for who actually buys. Cream and a serif read boutique and premium lifestyle, while this audience is price aware and institutional and wants a dependable trade catalogue; the deck admits the boutique risk. Worship and event buyers want energy or coverage proof, not catalogue calm. Cream can shift muddy on the cheap Android panels this audience uses, also admitted. And leaning the identity on "heritage since 1940" sits uncomfortably close to the one thing the brief forbids, sounding like the maker, even though the copy attributes the history correctly.
- differentiation 9. By far the most ownable and memorable; no competitor in Indian PA looks like this.
- accessibility 8. Honest numbers, re-checked. Risk is Fraunces at small sizes on warm cream on low end mobile, and a serif adds webfont weight.
- buildability 6. The heaviest Devanagari effort of the four, pairing two Noto families (Serif and Sans) against Fraunces and Inter, plus mandatory image re-masking onto white cards over cream.
- notAiLooking 9. The most distinctly human and anti template system; no default generator reaches for cream and Fraunces.
- adminFit 7. Dark walnut admin with a dark mode is fine, but the serif and cream identity does not extend naturally into a data dense console, so the admin is effectively a neutral separate skin.
- Flagged violation: sample headline 2, "Need the right amplifier for your hall? Tell us the area and we will match the wattage", and the same device in voice rule 3, open with a question used as a heading. The brief bans rhetorical question headings. The deck defends it as a real question answered in the same line, but it is still the closest any direction comes to breaking a hard copy rule, and it recurs.
- Total 43.

## 4. The Clean Technical Catalogue  (near white, charcoal, single blue accent, Inter plus IBM Plex Mono, parts catalogue behaviour)

- audienceFit 9. The most direct answer to AUDIENCE.md, which literally asks the store to behave like a parts catalogue where the fastest path to the right wattage, the right 100 V tap and a GST quote wins the order. Model number aware search matches the real Hinglish intent ("ahuja dpa 570m price"), tabular numerals align every price and spec, within family comparison tables and the wattage matcher serve schools, offices, integrators and procurement, and the near white surface is the familiar, trusted commerce look for the mobile price aware majority. Broadest coverage of the nine personas. The real cost: the one interactive colour is a blue drawn deliberately from the cobalt Ahuja badge, and that badge is prominent on the hero ALX-11000 and on the megaphone, so a blue UI beside a blue Ahuja badge flirts with reading as the manufacturer's own site. Three of the four directions independently refused manufacturer blue for exactly this reason; this one embraces it. It does not break the letter of the brief (it bans reusing the logo, not a colour, and the logo is replaced with an own three bar mark), but it works against the dealer not maker strategy and is the direction's chief risk to manage.
- differentiation 6. Deliberately like McMaster-Carr, Mouser and RS, which is honest but the least visually ownable of the four; near white plus blue plus Inter is the most common e-commerce look, and the deck admits it can read plain.
- accessibility 10. The most complete and rigorous: every semantic colour checked on surface, tints documented as carrying no contrast burden because dark text sits on them, a committed focus treatment (2px outline, 2px offset, 3px halo), a single accent that lowers cognitive load, and an actual inline SVG mark provided. All re-checked pairs pass.
- buildability 9. The tightest and simplest: one family for display and body (Inter) plus one mono (IBM Plex Mono) plus Noto, borders first with sparing shadow, self hosted subsets under 180 KB, a dark rail only in admin, and token plus lint rules proposed to forbid off system colours and non 6px radii.
- notAiLooking 7. The restraint is honestly reasoned and the copy is concrete, but near white plus blue plus Inter is also precisely what a generic template or an AI would default to, so it carries the lowest signal of a crafted, specific identity.
- adminFit 9. Excellent: a dark rail in both themes so the console never looks like the store, a full admin information architecture (Orders, Quotes, Products, Inventory, GST and Invoices), dark mode, and data grids that inherit the catalogue DNA directly.
- Total 50.

## Verdict

Winner: **The Clean Technical Catalogue**, narrowly over Specified by institutions (50 to 47).

It wins because it is the most faithful answer to who actually buys here. AUDIENCE.md describes a parts catalogue almost word for word, and this is the only direction built as one: model number aware search, tabular prices and specs that line up across a grid, within family comparison tables, the wattage and 100 V tap matcher front and centre, and a near white surface that the price aware, mobile first, Amazon and IndiaMART trained majority reads instantly as a real, safe shop. For both of my buyers the first five seconds land: the school secretary sees a datasheet grade store that will not misquote a spec and bills GST, and the event operator gets a fast, familiar, mobile path to price and stock. It is also the most buildable (two type families, the fewest token sets), the most rigorously and honestly accessible, and it has the best admin story. It breaks no hard rule: 6px radius, 44px buttons, 1600px, no eyebrows, no pills, no AI copy, no em dashes, and the manufacturer logo is replaced with its own three bar mark.

Its one serious flaw is strategic, not technical: choosing the manufacturer's own cobalt as the single interactive colour, placed next to the cobalt Ahuja badges in the photography, pulls against the dealer not maker positioning that is the spine of this brief. The fix is cheap and should be a launch condition: borrow the instinct the other three directions already proved, and shift the interactive blue clearly off the badge, cooler and darker (toward the institutional steely navy register) or to a non blue signal entirely, so the store never reads as Ahuja Radios the manufacturer. Pair that with one or two warmth moves on the worship, portable and event pages, which is where a near white catalogue feels most austere.

Specified by institutions is the strong runner up and the safe pick if the team wants the manufacturer-blue risk off the table on day one: it deliberately keeps its navy off the badge, reserves a warm marigold as a clearly-dealer signal, and has the single best admin console, at the cost of a more generic system and the most tokens to maintain. If Technical cannot resolve its blue cleanly, the order flips and Institutional should win.

### Best ideas to carry into the build regardless of which wins
- From Technical: the parts catalogue model number search, IBM Plex Mono tabular numerals for every price and spec, within family comparison tables, the wattage and 100 V tap matcher as a first class feature, borders first elevation, and token plus lint rules to police the system.
- From Institutional: the fill only discipline on the warm accent with its honest 3.19 note, the permitted flat line coverage and wiring diagrams, the operator dark mode admin with a marigold active bar, and the off badge steely navy reasoning.
- From Heritage: the pure white product card on a slightly warmer page so the photo's own white dissolves into the card, the three reds separated by register (matte ink vs emissive LED vs danger), and the most distinctly human, anti template instinct.
- From Stage: the single enforced light well colour, ratio and padding applied only by the product card component, and the sticky mobile Call, WhatsApp and Get quote bar as the loudest interactive element on every page.
